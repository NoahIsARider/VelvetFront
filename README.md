# Velvet Front

**A hand-drawn anti-war tactics game about rabbits who were told the war is holy.**

Velvet Front is a small top-down tactics campaign for Windows. You command a squad of Bluecap rabbit infantry through six operations of a holy war that the Ministry has already lost. Every battle is winnable and every victory makes the lie a little more useful to the people who ordered it.

The game is built with Electron, React and TypeScript. Backgrounds, characters, beasts, effect frames, panels and buttons are all hand-drawn PNG art, not front-end shapes.

![Velvet Front title screen](docs/screens/title.jpg)

## Download

**[Download the latest build](https://github.com/NoahIsARider/VelvetFront/releases/latest)** · Windows x64 · `Velvet-Front-0.9.2-win.zip` (about 240 MB)

Unzip it anywhere and run `Velvet Front.exe`. There is no installer, no account and no network access. The build is unsigned, so Windows SmartScreen will ask you to confirm the first launch.

## What it is

- Six chapters, each with its own painted battlefield, its own objective and its own way to fail.
- A field decision before every deployment. Mercy keeps rabbits alive, zeal keeps them moving, and both are recorded for the ending.
- Permanent consequences. Rabbits who die stay dead and are replaced by conscripts in later chapters.
- Three black contracts that rewrite the rules of a chapter for a score.
- A quartermaster that spends chapter clears on permanent upgrades.
- Keyboard, mouse and controller input, plus text scale, reduced motion and high contrast options.

## The campaign

Each order arrives as an atrocity with a tidy name. Collect four blood samples at Sweetberry River. Use the wounded as bait at the field hospital. Seal the nursery burrows before the young Moonhorns can escape.

![Campaign map](docs/screens/campaign.jpg)

The mission list is the only place the game shows you the whole war at once. Cleared chapters stay on the map with their thumbnails drained of colour, and the ledger underneath counts what the campaign has cost and what it has taught the Ministry.

## Story and decisions

Dialogue plays over the battlefield that is about to be fought on. Every speaker portrait is a painted asset from the same cast sheet as the sprites.

![Story dialogue](docs/screens/story.jpg)

At the end of each scene you choose a doctrine. The choice is mechanical, not cosmetic. Mercy restores health to the squad and adds medical charges. Zeal injects morale and hardens the enemy, and the campaign tracks which one you leaned on.

![Field decision](docs/screens/decision.jpg)

## Briefing

The briefing card repeats the order in the Ministry's own language, then tells you what actually fails the mission. The quote at the top is what command says out loud.

![Mission briefing](docs/screens/briefing.jpg)

## Battle

The battlefield is a painted plate with clickable terrain on top. Cover reduces incoming damage, mud slows movement, and blood roads are fast but drain morale every tick.

![Sweetberry River](docs/screens/battle-sweetberry.jpg)

Rabbits are selected with a click or `1` to `5`, moved with a click on open ground, and ordered to focus fire, treat wounds or retreat from the command bar. Moonhorns charge, circle and body-block corpses. The boss telegraphs a charge or a shock pulse before it lands.

![The Black Altar](docs/screens/battle-altar.jpg)

## Contracts and quartermaster

Contracts replay a chapter under worse rules. Double counterattacks, no field medicine, enemies at 135% health. Each one records a best score and awards a medal nobody should want.

![War contracts](docs/screens/contracts.jpg)

Supplies from first-time clears are spent on three permanent upgrades. They apply to every survivor for the rest of the campaign.

![Quartermaster](docs/screens/armory.jpg)

## Controls

| Action | Keyboard | Controller |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Select rabbit | `1` to `5` | LB |
| Move squad | Click, or arrow keys | D-pad |
| Focus fire | `F`, then click a Moonhorn | RB |
| Field medicine | `H` | X |
| Interact with objective | `E` | A |
| Retreat | `R` | B |
| Pause | `Space` | Start |

Shift-click adds a rabbit to the selection.

## Build from source

```bash
pnpm install
pnpm test        # 39 unit tests for simulation, terrain, objectives and campaign
pnpm desktop     # Vite dev server plus Electron
pnpm package:win # production bundle and the Windows zip in release/
```

The desktop bundle is loaded from `file://` inside the packaged app, so the build rewrites every `/assets` reference to a relative path. Without that step the packaged window renders with no art at all.

## Repository layout

```
electron/        main and preload process for the desktop shell
src/game/        simulation, terrain, objectives, waves, campaign, settings
src/App.tsx      screens, HUD, input and campaign flow
src/styles.css   layout and painted frame references
public/assets    drawn art, sliced sprites and audio
scripts/         sprite slicers, cropper and the audio generator
docs/            README screenshots and art plates
```
